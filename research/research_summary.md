# Research summary — *UFOs and Nukes* (Hastings, 2008)

*Generated 2026-11-15 (v1.9.0). Read this with `research_limitations.md`: ~27% of the book has been read verbatim (v1.0 pass + follow-up 1).*

## 1. Document reviewed
`f3ff02a2-hastings-2008-ufos-and-nukes.md` — OCR-converted markdown of Robert L. Hastings, *UFOs and Nukes: Extraordinary Encounters at Nuclear Weapons Sites* (24,203 lines, ~222,700 words; Introduction, 31 chapters, Appendices A–B, 358 parsed endnotes). No page numbers, photographs or figures are present in the file.

## 2. Executive summary
**Thesis (book).** Declassified FBI, CIA and Air Force records plus testimony from ~100 ex-USAF personnel show a persistent pattern of UFO activity at U.S. nuclear weapons sites from 1948 to 2006, including incidents in which ICBMs were disabled; the author argues the government conceals this (CLM001–CLM003).

**What the independent checks found (so far).**
1. *Real documents exist behind several headline claims* — the 31 Jan 1949 FBI “Protection of Vital Installations” memo, the Dec 1952 Chadwell CIA memo, the 1975 NORAD/NMCC messages, the Halt memo, and the 341st Strategic Missile Wing history. Quotations checked against secondary/transcribed copies matched (originals not yet obtained).
2. *The documents are less conclusive than the book’s framing.* The 341 SMW history (Apr–Jun 1967) records the Echo Flight shutdown at 0845 on 16 Mar 1967 as a “normal controlled shutdown”, says UFO rumors were “disproven”, notes power faults later that day and a similar Dec 1966 event, and says external-signal causes were judged “very remote” — it neither confirms nor rules out a non-power cause (SRC002, EVD001–EVD004).
3. *The testimony chronology is unstable.* The 1996 Klotz–Salas account places Salas at **November Flight on 16 March 1967**; the 2008 book places him at **Oscar Flight on “probably March 24”** (EVD005). The author acknowledges Salas had forgotten the flight designation.
4. *Big Sur (1964):* the main skeptical rebuttal (George 1993) is itself debatable on the launch date, but launch tables show the 15 Sep 1964 ABRES LORV-3 test as a **partial failure** — an untested alternative explanation for a warhead falling short (EVD008, EXP019). The film is reported destroyed; no primary data are available.
5. *Loring (Oct 1975):* the contemporaneous official wording describes an “unidentified helicopter” / “A/C”, not a UFO (EVD010).

**Bottom line (analyst assessment, not a book claim).** The strongest elements are *documented official concern and authentic records*; the weakest are the causal claims (“UFOs disabled missiles”), which rest on retrospective testimony with date/location inconsistencies and withheld or unavailable technical records. The evidence base is compatible with several mundane explanations that the book does not test.

## 3. Detailed document analysis (portions read)
**Structure.** Front matter and methods (lines 1–628): author’s story (father at Malmstrom; March 1967 radar anecdote, third-hand), interview methods (conversational leads, lecture circuit, email, AAFM newsletter 2002 → 37 replies, 7 useful), secrecy theory (witnesses “exempted” from NDAs; PRP chilling effect). Ch.1 argues radar and landing-trace data are “scientific evidence” and testimony “anecdotal.”
**Ch.2** 1948–52: Los Alamos/Sandia/Kirtland green fireballs and discs, Hanford, Oak Ridge, Savannah River, Killeen Base (Camp Hood), citing FBI 1949/1950 memos, La Paz, Ruppelt, McDonald (1968), Chadwell (1952).
**Ch.3** Nevada test shots: Webb’s 1981 phone interview with anonymous “Mr. M.” (18 discs, 30 Oct 1951), Dan Wilson’s fireball–fallout trajectory correlation hypothesis.
**Ch.6** Cuban Missile Crisis: acknowledges UFO activity was low; one anonymous Loring mechanic (“John Smith”) report with deliberately withheld details.
**Ch.9** Big Sur: Jacobs/Mansmann vs. Kingston George, launch-date argument (Sept 15 vs 22, 1964).
**Ch.10–11** Warren (1 Aug 1965, Blue Book log), Titan II Arizona 1962 (APRO), Malmstrom March 1967 (Echo/Oscar; Salas; Meiwald; Crawford; Gamble; wing history).
**Ch.18** 1975 Northern Tier: NORAD 24th Region log (7–10 Nov), NMCC, Haley, Freezeout Lake, Loring WSA.
**Ch.21** Bentwaters/Rendlesham: Halt memo, WSA “beams” first publicly mentioned 1991.

## 4. Critical analysis
**Supporting evidence.** Authentic FBI/CIA/Blue Book/NORAD documents; multiple named officers (Salas, Meiwald, Crawford, Halt); FOIA-released unit history confirming the Echo shutdown; physical-trace claims in Rendlesham.
**Weaknesses.**
- *Selection bias / denominator:* author actively sought UFO witnesses; he concedes most veterans reported nothing (CLM007) but no rate is estimated.
- *Retrospective memory:* decades-old recollections with admitted gaps (dates, flight names, personnel) — the author’s own “reliability” argument treats vividness as accuracy (CLM006, lines 535–577), which research on memory does not support (analyst note).
- *Anonymous/pseudonymous witnesses* (Mr. M., John Smith, Bentwaters WSA guards) preclude verification.
- *Editorializing in document presentation* (bracketed remarks in NORAD log, Ch.18) and statements like “confirmed beyond a reasonable doubt” (line 848) exceed what the documents show.
- *Unfalsifiable secrecy framing:* absence of records is interpreted as concealment (CLM008, CLM037).
- *Observer/reporting bias at sensitive sites* (analyst hypothesis — not tested in the book): guards at high-security facilities are numerous, vigilant and rewarded for reporting anomalies; a raw count of sightings at such sites needs a baseline.
**Contradictions identified.** November vs Oscar (EVD005); Mansmann’s agents CIA/three vs “government agents”/two (CLM042); Haley’s September/Minot-launch recollection vs documented late-Oct/Nov, Great Falls launches (lines 14216–14228); engineering report “never available” vs report dated 23 Mar 1967 cited in wing history (EVD006).
**Classification of content.** *Documented facts:* existence/dates of the FBI, CIA, NORAD, Halt, wing-history documents. *Attributed claims:* witness accounts. *Interpretations:* ET attribution, “warning” hypotheses (Salas, Jacobs), CIA involvement. *Speculation:* gravity/inertia neutralisation (CLM013), disinformation by critics (CLM046).

## Follow-up 2 (v1.9.0): Minot 24 Oct 1968 — recomputing the radar speed and checking the dispatcher log

**Sources obtained** (all from the Tulien/Sign Oral History Project site): Blue Book Final Case Report (primary reproduction, SRC025), Base Operations Dispatcher Log (SRC026), the 14 radarscope scans (SRC027), and the Shough (SRC023) and Poher (SRC024) analyses.

**Speed figures.** The file contains *two different official speed statements*: Werlich's 24 Oct memo (“2½ mile in 3 sec or about 3,000 mi/hr”) and his 29 Oct telex (echo closing from ~3 to ~1 mile, no speed). Clark's later “3,900” is, per Shough, **knots**, the simple mean of two paint-time-corrected co-altitude upper-limit rates (~1,990 kt for frames 771→772 and ~5,870 kt for 772→773). The book's derivation (“two miles in one three-second sweep”) would give 2,400 mph and is not how the number arose (EVD023, EVD024).

**My recomputation** from Shough's measured positions (EVD025): displacements 2.13 and 2.06 NM. Naive 3.0 s/frame → ~2,500 kt (~2,900 mph). Paint-time-corrected → ~2,020 and ~5,710 kt (matches Shough within ~3%); their simple mean reproduces ~3,900 kt, but the **time-weighted mean over the 5.1 s is only ~2,960 kt (~3,400 mph)**. These are upper limits that assume (i) one target, (ii) the same altitude as the B-52, (iii) an inferred range scale, and (iv) only 14 of probably >100 frames survive (EVD026). A sloping geometry lowers them (e.g., ~1,650 kt at 50° depression). So “a 3,900 mph object” is not a measurement.

**Timeline dispute (unresolved).** Shough: frames were taken *late* in the event; Poher: the radarscope clock runs ~9 min ahead, so they coincide with the early radio loss (EVD027). This changes how the fast transit should be related to the ground sightings.

**Dispatcher log vs. the Blue Book explanation.** The Blue Book text confirms the Oscar-7 sentence, the Sirius/B-52/plasma/Vega verdict, and that the B-52 flew upper airwork near the area from about the time of the first sighting; later sightings “correlate with” its VOR penetration/low approach/missed approach. But (EVD028): the 0828–0830Z jet-noise entries precede the B-52's low approach (~0904–0917 COM), and the 0940–1010Z entries, after the log says the B-52 landed, describe a stationary object *west* of N-7 near the ground. **Astronomical check** (EVD029): Sirius was at azimuth 132–162°, altitude 11–23° throughout, fitting the early “S/E of N-7” entries but not the later “west of N-7” ones; Vega was low in the NNW and set ~0940Z, while the pilot's light was “west-northwest”.

**Assessment.** The aircraft/star explanation plausibly covers some ground reports; it does not demonstrably cover the later stationary-object entries or the airborne radar echo. The book's speed derivation is wrong, and its account omits the Oscar-7 intrusion and the dispatcher-log/airwork correlation. Neither side's evidence yet reaches a conclusion: the key remaining items are the pilots' AF Forms 117, the approach transcript clock offsets and the original film.

## Follow-up 3 (v1.9.0): Minot 1968 — approach-control transcript, AF Forms 117, unified timeline

Full reconciled timeline: `documents/minot_1968_timeline.md`. Sources: transcript (SRC029) and five AF 117s (SRC028); Adams and Jablonski forms not viewed.

1. **Correction to my last report.** Shough and Poher do not really disagree on *when* the frames fall: both altitude models put them at ~10–11.5 k ft MSL, i.e., 3–5 minutes after the echo was first reported near FL200 and shortly before it was last reported (~10 k ft); they differ on the radarscope-clock offset (Shough 7–8 min, Poher 9, my linear-descent estimate 5–6). Consequence: the fast 771→773 transit is **not** the "initial" approach that Werlich's 3,000 mph memo and McCaslin describe, so the photos cannot test that speed (EVD034).
2. **Partin's own form vs. the book.** His 30 Oct 1968 questionnaire describes a stationary "bright orange ball of light," ~15 miles away at one o'clock, "on the ground or just slightly above," a "miniature sun placed on the ground." It does not mention an oblong shape, windows or a tube; those appear in later interviews (EVD035). His stated time (0430–0435 CDT) matches neither the transcript (downwind leg ≈0410 CDT) nor the file's note "should be 0335."
3. **Blue Book's Vega explanation fails on brightness.** Direction fits (≈328–332° predicted vs. Vega 336–337°), but at ~1° altitude Vega's atmospheric extinction makes it roughly magnitude 5–7, not a "bright orange ball" (EVD037). A star also cannot be passed "one mile to the south, and above it." A terrestrial light or other aircraft, with misjudged range, fits better but is untested (EXP029).
4. **Ground observers are not uniform.** Smith (burnt orange, twinkling like a star, on the horizon) and O'Connor (white→green→amber; file annotation "characteristic colors of Sirius"; last bearing ESE–SE at ~20°, matching Sirius) fit a scintillating star; Isley (headlight/landing-light-like lights plus a green flashing light) sounds more like an aircraft; Bond reports a point of light with fuzzy edges and a nearby town light (EVD038).
5. **What the transcript adds.** The 0835 "flying saucers again" line is real, but at 0852 the B-52 reported "nothing on our airborne radar" and "thick haze." No general-officer diversion appears in the transcript (a gap, not a refutation). A B-52 was near Minot at ~0828–0834Z, so the earliest "jet engines heard" entries may be it (correction to EVD028, see EVD040). An internal conflict remains: 0913Z "missile site personnel don't see anything anymore" vs. dispatcher entries of an object near N-7 until ~1010Z (EVD039).

**Assessment.** The aircraft/star explanation covers part of the ground evidence better than I credited last time (jet-noise entries, Sirius-like descriptions) but fails for the B-52 air visual as Vega, and does not explain the airborne radar echo or the post-landing dispatcher entries. The book's later-interview details (windows, oblong shape) are not in the 1968 form. Still unresolved: the echo's true speed (assumption-bound), the clock offset, and the missing film.

## Follow-up 4 (v1.9.0): remaining Minot case-file documents and independent frame measurement

**Read:** Blue Book docs 3, 5–7, 9–13, 15, 25–27, 29 (typed memos/TWXs) and all seven AF Forms 117 (viewed as scans). Not read: docs 1, 2, 4, 14 (upper-air data), 30–31 (maps), and Werlich's own 29 Oct report; no OCR tool was available for scanned-only pages.

**What the file adds (SRC030, EVD041–EVD047)**
1. **Origin of "3,000 mph":** a same-day telephone relay from Werlich to FTD (doc 5): "2½ mile in 3 sec or at about 3,000 mi/hr." It is not a photo-derived value.
2. **Werlich's own view (doc 12, 31 Oct):** "Most of the original sightings were of the aircraft … almost 80 per cent were looking at the B-52"; the unidentified item is the radar/aircraft event, which "couldn't be a malfunction because they transmitted before and afterwards." He also says the scope camera was started late ("towards the end"), supporting Shough's placement of the frames. The book uses his data but not this assessment.
3. **Oscar-7 intrusion:** three earlier padlock/combination incidents had been traced to Air Police; "99 chances out of 100" someone with a key; no tracks found. The book omits both the intrusion and these precedents.
4. **Command pressure on Blue Book (doc 10):** HQ SAC's Col Pullen asked Quintanilla to "get this thing simmered down" and to "hit a little heavy on what has happened to other aircraft … this would help play the issue down." Quintanilla sent no one to investigate (four-person staff) and issued a preliminary "plasma / celestial bodies" TWX (doc 25) calling the reports "fairly routine" before raw data arrived. This supports the book's theme of a hasty, command-influenced evaluation, though docs 11–13 show Blue Book also tried to obtain data.
5. **Smith's "separation":** a Blue Book staff note says "Smith does not say this on 117"; my reading of his form agrees. Isley, Adams and Jablonski do describe two lights with one disappearing (EVD045, EVD051).
6. **Weather radar:** the transcript says the weather radar saw the target, but Blue Book calls on 8 and 13 Nov to ask about it went unanswered; no weather-radar record is in the file I read (EVD046).

**Independent measurement of the radarscope frames (SRC031, EVD048–EVD050).** From the 300-ppi scans I calibrated the rings (frame 771: 60/134/208 px, 74 px = 0.5 NM; hole edge 266 px = 2.14 NM, matching Shough's 2.1) and found the strongest echo in each of frames 772 and 773 (1.09 and 1.08 NM; Shough 1.05), with the same 158° angular separation. The 772→773 displacement is **2.14 NM** (Shough 2.06, +4%). I could **not** recover the frame-771 echo (it overlaps the third ring), so the 771→772 rate rests on Shough. The range calibration (no scale indicator on the photos) matters: without the short-range offset the displacement would be 1.46 NM (~1,750 kt at 3 s, ~4,040 kt at 1.3 s). Script: `database/build/analysis/minot_frame_measure.py`.

**Assessment.** The documentary record now looks like this: a real, unexplained airborne radar/radio event (Werlich: not a malfunction; Shough: no conventional explanation fully convincing), plus a ground-sighting complex that Werlich himself thought mostly the B-52 and that Blue Book attributed mainly to aircraft and Sirius, with an evaluation process visibly shaped by SAC pressure. The book's claims are best supported on the radar/radio event and the handling of the case, and weakest on the ground sightings as evidence of a single hovering craft and on the stated speed.

## Follow-up 5 (v1.9.0): F.E. Warren, 1 Aug 1965 — the "stars" explanation — and Chapter 16

**Read:** book lines 8851–9481 (remaining Warren material, Hynek/Fowler 1966 Minot) and all of Chapter 16 (12987–13288). **Not obtained:** the original Anspaugh log (the book and Hynek's 1972 printing are the only versions I have).

### Warren, 31 Jul–2 Aug 1965
1. **The book omits the wing's own report.** The 90th SMW report to FTD (reproduced on Hastings's site, SRC032) counts **148 objects reported by 143 personnel** over three nights (75/70, 29/27, 44/46), described as mostly round, pinhead-to-nickel sized, with colored lights flashing every 1–2 seconds, V/echelon/cross/box groupings, silent (EVD052). Those descriptors and counts are not in the book. A 20 Sep 1965 Blue Book addendum (SRC033) adds binocular and theodolite observations and, on four occasions, "a roaring jet-like noise" (EVD053).
2. **Blue Book's handling was weak but not a single "stars" verdict.** The file lacks positional data, photos or triangulation; Blue Book labelled the whole flap "inversion effect," assigned no case-by-case identifications, and a Blue Book sergeant's request for positional data went unheeded (a skeptic's review, SRC034, EVD054). The book's claim that Blue Book called them "twinkling stars" rests on Hynek's account of Quintanilla (CLM100, not yet checked in *The UFO Experience*).
3. **My sky test is inconclusive because the log has no time zone** (EVD055–EVD056). If the Anspaugh times are Mountain, Jupiter (mag −1.9), Aldebaran and Capella were rising in the east/north-east at 0317–0340, the "70° azimuth" entry matches Jupiter (71°), and "four NW / three NE" at 0400 fits Vega/Deneb and Capella/Jupiter. But the 0130–0250 entries have no Jupiter, and if the times are Eastern (Wright-Patterson), every entry falls before Jupiter rose and the match fails. The log's own timing is internally odd (calls after events, out of order).
4. **Net:** the stars/planets/satellites/aircraft + inversion + group-suggestion explanation (EXP032) is plausible for much of the flap, especially the mass-report pattern, but cannot be shown entry by entry; the book's "patently absurd" is stronger than the evidence allows, and so is Blue Book's blanket attribution.
5. **Witness accounts** (Tashner, Earnshaw, Stuck, Thompson) are second-hand or undated; Thompson's "NORAD tracking eight objects" has no documentation (CLM103).

### Chapter 16
- **Bolender document:** the key sentence is verbatim (“…which could affect national security are made in accordance with JANAP 146 or Air Force Manual 55-11, and are not part of the Blue Book system (Atch 10)”), but NICAP calls it a **draft**, not a signed memorandum to all commands; the same text says there is no evidence Blue Book reports served an intelligence function; the 16 attachments are missing (EVD057).
- **"Blue Book dismissed Minot as having no merit"** is not what the file shows (aircraft/Sirius/plasma attributions, SAC pressure — EVD058).
- Unchecked: Ruppelt's "63 cases" file, Aldrich's 4 Nov 1948 USAFE cable, OSI-never-reached-Blue-Book (CLM097–CLM099).

## Follow-up 6 (v1.9.0): the Warren log's time zone and a redone star-by-star test

**Found:** Hynek's printing of the log (*The UFO Experience*, 1972, pp. 233–236; archive.org full text, SRC037). The book's version matches it.

1. **Time zone (EVD059).** Hynek introduces the entries as calls "received at the Blue Book offices by Lieutenant Anspaugh" in Dayton. The batch of events relayed in the 4:40 call (3:00, 3:17, 3:20–3:40) all precede that call, which only works if they are on the same clock; if they were Warren local (Mountain) they would postdate it. So the log is most probably on the **Eastern** clock (EDT vs EST unresolved), not Mountain.
2. **Correction to my last report (EVD061).** I said the star match "fails" under Eastern time. That was wrong: I had omitted the Pleiades and Aldebaran. Under Eastern readings: "white oval directly overhead" (3:00) → **Deneb** at 78–86° altitude; "seven UFOs east" (3:20) and "six stacked vertically" (3:25) → the **Pleiades** (six–seven naked-eye stars) low in the ENE; "one at 70° azimuth" (3:40) → Pleiades/Aldebaran at az 68–77°; "three NE" (4:00) → Capella/Pleiades; "NW" → Vega and Deneb. Jupiter matters only if the times are Mountain, which is now unlikely.
3. **What stars do not explain (EVD062).** The 3:17 "oval and white with white lines on its sides and a flashing red light in its center … descending … landed 10 miles east" (aircraft-like); the "120°" entry (no bright object there); "four NW" (only two bright NW stars); "three came from the east … passed through the other two, all five heading west" (needs illusory motion or aircraft); and the 1:30 "circular object emitting several colors" moving NE.
4. **Hynek's own reading (EVD060).** He confirms Blue Book's "stars seen through inversion layers" label and Quintanilla's "nothing but stars!" (CLM100 now matches his text), argues inversions cannot move stars through a large arc, then sets out both a cover-up hypothesis and a "foul-up" hypothesis and concludes "Such lack of interest belies any charge of cover-up; they just didn't care." The book uses the quote but not that conclusion.
5. **Not done (EVD063):** Echo 2 and other satellite passes — no 1965 orbital elements were available; satellites remain an untested candidate for the moving "UFOs."

**Revised assessment.** For the Warren flap, a stars-and-planets explanation (Deneb, the Pleiades, Capella, Vega) plausibly accounts for a larger share of the log than I credited last time, especially under the likely Eastern clock; it leaves a minority of aircraft-like or motion-based entries unexplained (and satellites/aircraft/autokinesis untested). The book's "patently absurd" overstates it; Blue Book's blanket "stars" also overstates it. The most probable truth is a mix of stars, aircraft, possibly satellites, and group suggestion, but the file lacks the positional data to say so entry by entry.

## Follow-up 7 (v1.9.0): the Echo 2 satellite test for the Warren log

**Data found without any user input:** the Smithsonian Astrophysical Observatory's published orbital elements for 1965 (SAO Special Report 289, [NTRS 19700026243](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19700026243/downloads/19700026243.pdf), SRC038): smoothed elements for Echo 2 (16 Jul–16 Aug 1965) and daily mean elements for Echo 1 (August 1965). (Echo 2 is NORAD 740; an early search used the wrong catalog number.)

**Result (EVD064–EVD067).** Over Cheyenne on 1 Aug 1965 (UTC) Echo 2 made three visible passes (sunlit, sky dark): 0502–0508Z (peak 10°, east→north-east), **0650–0659Z (peak 66°, rising ESE, peaking east, ending north)** and 0839–0848Z (peak 25°, west→north-north-west). The two element sets agree to ~10 s. Echo 1 had no visible pass. In printed-clock terms the passes fall at 23:02/00:50/02:39 (Mountain), 00:02/01:50/03:39 (EST) or 01:02/02:50/04:39 (EDT).

**Does it explain the log?** Not diagnostically. With ±3 min slack, 1 entry matches under Mountain, 3 under EST (1:48, 1:49, 3:40) and 3 under EDT (2:50, 3:00, and the 4:40 call). A random clock shift gives ~1.9 of 18 entries matched by chance (P(≥3) ≈ 0.26). Echo 2 is a single, silent, steadily moving light crossing the sky in minutes, so it is only a candidate for isolated "moving light" entries (e.g., "nine more UFOs" at 2:50 or "overhead" at 3:00 under EDT), not for formations, stacking or crossing paths.

**Self-correction.** My first run had a date-conversion sign error that put the Sun ~90 days off; I caught it from an implausible Sun altitude and all results above are from the fixed code.

**Cumulative assessment of the 1 Aug 1965 Warren log.** Stars/planets (Deneb, the Pleiades, Capella, Vega) plausibly explain many entries under the likely Eastern clock; Echo 2 adds possible explanations for one or two moving-light entries at chance-level significance; a minority (flashing-red-light oval, "120° azimuth," crossing/stacking motions) remain unexplained by anything tested. The file's lack of positional data prevents entry-by-entry identification.

## Follow-up 8 (v1.9.0): other satellites, faint stars, and what remains untested for the Warren log

- **1964-5A (Saturn SA-5 S-IV stage):** no visible pass over Cheyenne that night; it orbits at ~400 km and was in Earth's shadow except for one daylight pass (EVD068). Explorer 19, Explorer 8, Vanguard 2/3, Anna 1B and Injun 3 are too faint to be naked-eye reports.
- **Correction to my "120° azimuth" point (EVD069):** I said no bright object lay there; true, but fainter stars (mag 2.0–2.5) do lie within ±15° of azimuth 120° under each clock reading (e.g., Alpheratz/Sirrah under EST). The entry gives no elevation or brightness, so it is not diagnostic either way.
- **Still untested (EVD070):** the Pegasus series and their S-IV stages (Pegasus 3, 1965-060A, was launched 30 Jul 1965 — the day before the flap began — and its elements are not in any source I could retrieve), meteors, and aircraft (the 3:17 flashing-red-light "oval … descending … moving east" is aircraft-like).

**Where this leaves the 1 Aug 1965 Warren log:** a plausible natural-explanation set (stars/planets under the likely Eastern clock; Echo 2 for a couple of entries at chance level) with a handful of entries still not explained or testable with available data. Read the rest of Ch.21 (lines 16061–17362; coverage 82.9%) and the Halt memo itself (SRC070). 5 incidents (INC123–INC127), 4 witness records, 7 claims. Findings (EVD135–EVD136):

- **Weapons removal and radar** are anonymous or unlogged recollections (Burris saw only a guarded C-5A; Halt calls such movements routine; the controllers disagree on direction, a right-angle turn and a visual; Eastern Radar's 28 Dec 0325 GMT log shows a flashing light and nothing on radar).
